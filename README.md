## 🎯 Business Problem

IKEA Gifting is a UK-based, non-store online retailer specializing in all-occasion giftware and home decor. A meaningful share of its customers are **wholesale buyers**, which complicates how customer value should be read.

Leadership has no structured, data-driven view of which customers drive the most revenue or which are quietly disengaging. Retention and marketing efforts have been **reactive rather than targeted**, and spend isn't prioritized toward the customers most likely to respond.

## 🎯 Objective

Identify the most valuable customer segments and quantify churn risk across the customer base, so Marketing, CRM, and Retention teams can focus effort and spend on the customers who matter most, instead of applying a uniform strategy to everyone.

## ❓ Business Questions

1. Who are our most valuable customers, and how much revenue do they actually contribute?
2. Which customers have gone quiet and are at risk of churning entirely?
3. How do customer value and retention trend over time (month-over-month, year-over-year)?
4. How do customer value and churn risk differ across geographic markets?
5. What concrete actions should the business take for each segment?

## 📦 Dataset

- **Source:** [Online Retail II – UCI Machine Learning Repository](https://archive.ics.uci.edu/dataset/502/online+retail+ii) (donated by Daqing Chen)
- **Size:** 1,067,371 transaction rows
- **Period:** December 2009 – December 2011
- **Fields:** invoice, product description, quantity, unit price, customer ID, invoice date, country
- A backup copy is kept in this repo under `data/raw/` in case the source becomes unavailable.

## 🔄 Methodology

```
Raw data (UCI)  →  SQL cleaning & dedup  →  Python RFM scoring  →  Churn flag  →  Power BI dashboard
```

1. **Extract & clean (SQL):** remove duplicates, handle missing customer IDs, filter cancellations/returns, compute line revenue.
2. **RFM scoring (Python):** calculate Recency, Frequency, Monetary per customer; score each dimension; map score combinations to segments.
3. **Churn flag:** fixed 90-day inactivity threshold.
4. **Visualize (Power BI):** multi-page interactive dashboard with slicers for Month, Quarter, Year, Country, Segment, and IsChurned.

## 📖 Key Definitions

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Recency** | Days since a customer's most recent purchase |
| **Frequency** | Number of distinct purchase transactions in the analysis period |
| **Monetary** | Total revenue generated by a customer in the analysis period |
| **Churn** | No recorded purchase activity in the trailing 90 days |

## 👥 Customer Segments

| Segment | Description |
|---|---|
| 🏆 **Champions** | High recency, frequency, and monetary value – the most valuable customers |
| 💙 **Loyal** | Consistent purchase history with moderate-to-high value |
| ⚠️ **At Risk** | Previously valuable customers whose recency has recently dropped |
| 🆕 **New** | Recent first-time customers, not yet repeat buyers |
| 👀 **Needs Attention** | Moderate recency but low purchase frequency |
| 💤 **Lost** | No meaningful activity for an extended period |

## 🔍 Key Findings

- **Revenue is highly concentrated.** Champions generate about **$12.1M of ~$18M total revenue (~68%)**, far ahead of Loyal ($2.7M) and At Risk ($1.6M).
- **Churn is high.** **50.9%** of the 5,881 customers have had no purchase in the trailing 90 days.
- **At Risk revenue is material.** About **$1.6M** sits with previously valuable customers whose recency is slipping, making it the clearest win-back opportunity.
- **Revenue peaks in Q4.** Q4 revenue (~$6.5M) is nearly double Q1 (~$3.4M), with a steady build from September onward, consistent with gifting seasonality.
- **Year-over-year revenue is roughly flat** between 2010 ($8.7M) and 2011 ($8.3M). 2009 covers only December, and 2011 data ends in December, so full-year comparisons need care.
- **Country performance varies widely.** The Netherlands and France show the strongest average Recency and Frequency scores in the visible country table, while EIRE leads on average customer value (~$124K), likely driven by a small number of wholesale accounts.

## ✅ Recommended Actions by Segment

| Segment | Recommended Action |
|---|---|
| 🏆 Champions | Protect and reward: early access, loyalty perks, personal account management for top wholesale buyers |
| 💙 Loyal | Upsell and cross-sell; nudge toward Champion behavior with bundles and tiered incentives |
| ⚠️ At Risk | Priority win-back: personalized outreach, time-limited offers, and feedback requests before they lapse |
| 🆕 New | Onboarding journey and a second-purchase incentive to build repeat behavior |
| 👀 Needs Attention | Low-cost re-engagement: reminders, product recommendations, seasonal campaigns |
| 💤 Lost | Low-cost reactivation test only; suppress from high-cost campaigns if there is no response |

## ⚠️ Assumptions & Limitations

- Churn uses a **fixed 90-day threshold** for simplicity and interpretability, not a statistically derived cutoff.
- **Wholesale and retail customers are not separated**, since the data doesn't clearly distinguish them. High-value outliers (e.g., EIRE) may reflect wholesale accounts.
- Analysis is based on **historical transactions only**; promotions, marketing calendar, and external seasonality drivers are not modeled.
- **Out of scope:** predictive churn modeling, product-level recommendations, real-time scoring, and campaign execution.
